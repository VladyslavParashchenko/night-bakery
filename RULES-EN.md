# Night Bakery — rules

Night, a bakery, three ovens and a mountain of dough. Whoever bakes off all
their pastries first is the king of the bakery.

## The deck (72 cards)

- **52 pastry cards** — like a regular deck of playing cards, but instead of
  suits there are 4 pastries, each with its own colour: 🥐 croissant — coral,
  🥯 bagel — turquoise, 🥨 pretzel — purple, 🍩 donut — yellow.
  Each suit has 13 cards with values from **2 to 14** (11–13 stand in for the
  Jack, Queen and King, 14 for the Ace).
- **16 cook cards** — 4 kinds, each in each of the 4 colours:
  - **Scrubber** — empties an oven.
  - **Lock** — locks an oven until your next turn.
  - **Spy** — swaps a random card with an opponent you choose.
  - **Carousel** — every player passes one card to the left.
- **4 Chefs** — colourless cooks: they go into any oven and give it a
  colour.

## Setup

1. 2 to 5 players.
2. Shuffle the deck and deal **7 cards** to each player.
3. Turn one pastry card from the deck face up into each of the **three
   ovens**. If a cook comes up, put it back into the deck, shuffle and turn
   over another card.
4. The rest of the deck stays face down. The discard pile goes next to it.

## Your turn

On your turn you may play **up to 3 cards** — pastries and cooks in any
combination and order. Put each card into any oven where it fits.

### Drawing at the end of the turn

The quota is **3 cards per turn**. When you end your turn, draw as many cards
from the deck as you fell short of three:

| Played this turn | You draw |
| ---------------- | -------- |
| 3                | 0        |
| 2                | 1        |
| 1                | 2        |
| nothing          | 3        |

A player who plays the last card from their hand wins at once (see
"Winning").

### Putting pastries into an oven

- Any pastry card can go into an empty oven.
- On a non-empty oven, play a card of **the same colour** or **the same
  value** as the oven's top card. For example, on a turquoise 5 you can play
  any turquoise card or any 5.
- If you play several cards into one oven, each next card is compared with
  the new top card: turquoise 5 → yellow 5 → yellow 8 → coral 8.

### Burning ovens

When an oven reaches **5 cards**, it burns: all its cards go to the discard
pile, and the player who put in the fifth card **draws 2 penalty cards**. If
they still have plays left in the chain, they may keep putting cards into
this (now empty) oven.

### Signature recipe

If you play **3 pastry cards of the same value** in one turn, each opponent
draws 1 card.

### Scorched!

Playing a card of **the same value** as the top card (for example, a purple
7 on a turquoise 7) is allowed but risky. Matching by colour is the safe
play. After a turn in which this happened, a short window opens: any other
player who is first to call "Scorched!" makes the culprit draw 1 card (one
per turn, no matter how many such cards were played).

## Cooks

A cook is also a card of a certain colour. It goes into an oven whose top
card is **the same colour**, or into an empty oven. A cook has no value, so
it can't be played by value. The exception is the **Chef**: it is colourless
and goes into any oven.

The cook stays in the oven as its top card: the next card on it must be the
same colour. It counts towards the five cards, so it can burn the oven.

- **Scrubber**: the oven it was put into is emptied — all its cards,
  including the Scrubber itself, go to the discard pile.
- **Lock**: the oven it was put into is locked — until the start of your
  next turn other players can't put cards into it.
- **Spy**: choose an opponent — a random card of yours goes to them, and a
  random card of theirs comes to you. Nobody chooses which cards. If either
  of you has no cards, there is no swap.
- **Carousel**: every player who has cards secretly picks one and passes it
  to their left-hand neighbour.
- **Chef**: put it into any oven, regardless of the top card, and pick a
  colour. The Chef stays in the oven as that colour, so the next card on it
  must be the chosen colour. When the oven is emptied or burns, the Chef
  becomes colourless again.

Cooks can't be put into an oven locked by someone else's Lock either.

## Out of cards

Shuffle the discard pile — that is the new deck. If the discard pile is empty
too, no cards are drawn.

## Winning

The game ends as soon as a player has no cards left in hand, even mid-turn.
That player wins. The last card still takes effect first: if it burns an
oven, the player draws the penalty cards and the game goes on. No "Scorched!"
window opens after the winning card, and a Carousel played as the last card
no longer passes any cards.

## Scoring

When the game ends, the other players score penalty points for the cards
left in their hands:

| Card                                 | Points          |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------- |
| Pastry                               | its value, 2–14 |
| Cook (Scrubber, Lock, Spy, Carousel) | 15              |
| Chef                                 | 20              |

The winner has 0 points. The other places go by points: fewer is better.
Equal points share a place, so a player whom a Spy or Carousel also left
empty-handed shares first place with the winner.

With three or more players, whoever has the most points is the worst baker
of the game. If several players tie for the most, they all are.
